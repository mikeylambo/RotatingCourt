# Rotating Court: playtest sheet (gate before the Deceiver edit)

Live build: https://rotating-court.vercel.app/ (commit shown in the Vercel deployment list). Play at least **3 full Reigns** (about 2 to 3 minutes each if you stop quickly).

## Play it three ways
1. **Run 1, normal.** Phone in portrait if you can, otherwise desktop. Tap or press Space anywhere to stop the wheel.
2. **Run 2, push the wheel.** Get past day 15 if you can. Notice how the pointer speeds up with the day and with Population.
3. **Run 3, your usual device.** If run 1 was desktop, use touch (or a gamepad). Try pause, Settings → Assist, and quitting then using Continue.

## What to write down (a line each is plenty)
- **Wheel feel.** Does stopping feel fair when you miss? Does the stop land where you aimed? Any run where it felt like the wheel cheated you?
- **Speed.** Too slow on day 1? Impossible by day 15 to 25? (Day 1 YES window is 760 ms, day 25 worst case is about 264 ms.)
- **Readability.** Can you tell YES, NO and the hazard apart at a glance, and without relying on colour? Can you read the ask icon and the one line?
- **Hazards.** Does getting hit feel like a consequence you understand, or random?
- **Delayed effects.** Did you notice the calendar strip? Did a result ever surprise you in a good way?
- **Input.** Any missed taps, double stops, or a stop that fired too early after a result?
- **Anything broken**, with what you were doing.

## Quick facts so you know what is intended
- Win: survive day 25 with all three stats above the flag on each bar. Lose: any stat hits zero.
- Assist (Settings) slows the wheel and widens segments; scoring is unchanged. Auto-stop lets you pick Yes or No with no timing and marks the run as assisted.
- Saves: your run resumes via Continue after a reload; settings persist.

## Not covered yet
Real-device performance, audio on a phone, and PWA install have not been tested.
